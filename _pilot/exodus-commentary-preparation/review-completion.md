# Preparation for the Exodus commentary: completed review

11 October 2026. Prepared for Moshe Kline. This is a record of preparation, not an Exodus commentary.

## What the review establishes

The preparatory review of the identified Genesis commentary corpus and the principal methodological works is complete. It includes the nineteen current Genesis unit commentaries, the book-level and collected Genesis treatments, the Akedah studies, the major Torah-wide manuscripts, and the methodological studies of the Decalogue, Leviticus, the Beautiful Weave, the Covenant Code and the Mishnah. The final pass through Individual Books covered the remaining selected Deuteronomy, Leviticus and Numbers articles, structural outlines and teaching notes.

The purpose was to learn both the explicit principles and the practice of interpretation: how a question becomes a structural argument, how the argument becomes a reading, and how the reader is brought into that discovery.

This is not a claim that every file in the TORAH archive was read independently. Repeated texts were compared, with changed passages read in context; representative versions were used for additional draft families. Reference Bibles, secondary literature, machine translations and correspondence were distinguished from authored commentary. Nor does reading an argument certify every historical claim, Hebrew derivation or diagram. The detailed records preserve these limits rather than treating discovery or extraction as reading.

## The method to carry into Exodus

**Begin with the text's problem.** The commentary should open with a concrete difficulty: an unexpected juxtaposition, repeated episode, change of vocabulary, interruption or apparently misplaced law. The arrangement should answer that difficulty. A diagram becomes useful when the reader understands what it explains.

**Establish the unit before interpreting its architecture.** Use converging boundaries, repeated formulas, closures, narrative transitions and internal organization. Account for the complete passage. Distinguish the evidence that identifies a collection from the evidence that orders its members and from the interpretation of that order.

**Read the rows and columns together.** A cell belongs to more than one context. Its content should reflect both its row and its column; a row pair may add a third coordinate. Shared language establishes a comparison, while the difference between counterparts often supplies its meaning. Position must explain something that a list of parallels does not.

**Derive the categories locally.** Self/other, individual/community, private/public, immanent/transcendent and natural/supernatural are related but not interchangeable. The manuscripts repeatedly refine their terminology. A conceptual middle must be shown to share different features with its two poles. It need not mean compromise, and the physical center need not always have the same function.

**Preserve sequence and scale.** A spatial reading adds contexts to narrative sequence. It does not erase sequence. Distinguish the arrangement of a pericope from that of a unit, book or whole Torah. Specify reading direction and the hierarchy of subdivisions. Physical left/right changes between Hebrew and English displays; labels must remain unambiguous.

**Treat names, voices and acts precisely.** Preserve YHWH, Elohim, haElohim and compound forms where they matter. Distinguish the narrator's language, a character's invocation, reported speech and direct divine action. A character's understanding need not equal the narrator's. The manuscripts' differentiation of divine names is explicitly compatible with their referring to one deity.

**Let details constrain interpretation.** Exact Hebrew words, grammatical forms and repeated formulas take priority over convenient English synonyms. Keep lexical correspondence, sound play and thematic analogy distinct. Explain exceptions; do not remove or regularize them to perfect a pattern. A translation adjusted to display a relationship must disclose the adjustment.

**Invite discovery in the prose.** Follow the Genesis commentaries' gradual movement from puzzle to arrangement to meaning. Use connected argument, precise quotations and a restrained “we” that invites inspection. Give the strongest evidence first and introduce figures before expecting readers to interpret them. Return to the opening difficulty with a clear account of what has been explained.

**Keep literary findings and historical proposals distinct.** Final-form coherence, intentional composition, a single individual author, a coordinated school, dating, oral transmission and deliberate concealment are different claims. The sources vary in their confidence. Their more tentative formulations should remain visible; neither an editor's encouragement nor a reviewer's objection is automatically the author's position.

## What the final individual-books pass added

The Hebrew Beautiful Weave studies clarify a third dimension: each row belongs to a pair, and each pair belongs to a progression. They also demonstrate how a passage that looks like an interruption can disclose the composition's organizing principle. This provides a practice to test in Exodus, not a ready-made set of labels to transfer there.

The Deuteronomy outlines show the importance of speaker, addressee, singular/plural forms and different versions of a repeated narrative. Their alternative maps and unfinished headings are working proposals, not interchangeable final results. The “V” materials extend the comparative experiment; their claims about antiquity or authenticity are not established by the structural comparisons alone.

The Leviticus notes model unusually useful restraint: the consecration comparison explicitly acknowledges a counterpart for which no direct parallel has been demonstrated. The editorial exchanges also stress explaining figures, qualifying assertions and distinguishing verbal evidence from conceptual interpretation. Proof corrections identify accidental highlighting, a reminder that color in an archival file is not automatically an analytical claim.

The Numbers teaching material begins with the placement of laws inside narrative and tests the proposed encampment arrangement against that problem. Its contrast between narrative movement and a stable spatial image helps clarify what a book-level map can contribute. The chronological “black hole” is a literary analogy, not an independently dated event.

## Matters to keep explicit when writing begins

1. **Exodus XIII/XIV have competing archival boundaries.** The current tables put XIII at 29:1–37 and XIV at 29:38–31:17. An older full Exodus arrangement extends XIII through 30:10 and begins XIV at 30:11. Another older overview already uses the current division. This is a version issue, not a missing passage, and should not be described as a simple chronological progression from old to new.
2. **The signs require local verification.** Do not confuse the middle row with the middle column. Check each agent, gesture and hardening formula against its actual verse before repeating an overview's generalization.
3. **Genesis comparisons require their full context.** Creation/completion, clothing, recognition, covenant, family and divine presence are promising links. Their significance must be demonstrated in the Exodus passage; the Genesis row scheme is not an Exodus template.
4. **Holiness and presence are questions to investigate.** The manuscripts suggest a movement from concealed divine action toward a dwelling among Israel. That supplies a guiding question, not a conclusion to impose on every unit.
5. **Drafting will include verification.** Preparation is complete, but each new unit commentary still needs its exact Hebrew evidence, displayed arrangement, boundaries and citations checked as its argument is developed.
