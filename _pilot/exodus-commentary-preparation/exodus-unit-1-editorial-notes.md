## Interpretive distinctions retained

The main argument takes the author's national/individual distinction as its basis. In Row 3 both columns contain Moses and both concern Israel; the labels describe emphases. Row 2 is a conceptual bridge through shared divine attention, not a third column or a symmetrical block of equal length.

The “Who made you?” / “Who am I?” opening is this draft's organizing interpretation, grounded in 2:14 and 3:11. It is not presented as a quotation from a previously completed Exodus commentary. Likewise, the female rescue sequence, the representation argument and the contrasts between the signs develop the displayed evidence into a continuous reading.

The future sign in 3:12 and the practical signs in 4:1–9 are distinguished without claiming the third sign was performed at the bush. The water-to-blood instruction is a promised sign. The rod becomes a נחש here; it is not conflated with the תנין in the later confrontation.

The draft does not equate YHWH/Elohim with separate gods or claim one name is confined to one column. Elohim acts for the midwives in Row 1; that row is not described as a world without divine action. Revelation and naming are not used as evidence that the name was absent from Genesis.

3Ad begins inside 3:21 with והיה כי תלכון לא תלכו ריקם. A table that labels it merely 3:22 would omit part of the displayed subdivision. 4:18 closes the current unit even though the Masoretic paragraph marker occurs after 4:17.

Genesis creation language and the tevah comparison are modest literary connections, not a claim that the entire Genesis matrix is reproduced here. Further book-level correspondences can be developed after the succeeding Exodus units are written; they are not prerequisites imposed on this first unit.
