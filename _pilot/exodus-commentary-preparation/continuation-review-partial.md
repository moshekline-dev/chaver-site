## Principles sharpened by these manuscripts

1. Context includes both immediate sequence and the demonstrated relationships of the larger arrangement. A reading should integrate the actual details rather than impose a category from outside.
2. Distinguish rings/focal symmetry from tables; they can coexist but are not interchangeable. Demonstrate each level independently.
3. The conceptual middle is understood through the poles, but its priority in meaning need not coincide with its order of exposition or chronological position.
4. Keep separate the rule defining a collection, the rule ordering its members, and the further interpretation of that order. *Ways of Holiness* explicitly calls inference from three exemplars speculative and distinguishes subjective interest from the more objective test of integration.
5. Exceptions can refine a proposed category, but must be accounted for explicitly. Do not make universal claims about a column when only some members support the proposed theme.
6. Reading direction and displayed position must be specified independently. The Beautiful Weave uses column-wise sequence; Hebrew and English reverse physical left/right. Do not assign a permanent spatial meaning to a letter or to warp/weft across every scale.
7. The long monograph explicitly says YHWH and Elohim name the same deity, analogous to Jacob/Israel. Its dramatic differentiation does not warrant describing two independent gods. Narration, quoted speech, names and active appearances remain separate evidence categories.
8. The monograph's whole-Torah tapestry afterword calls its explanation hypothetical and leaves several questions for future study. Carry forward the proposal with that qualification.
9. The Beautiful Weave offers a third coordinate beyond row and column: the row pair's position in the sequence of five pairs. Agricultural progression and the four strongly linked edge pairs should be shown before discussing the garment/house interpretation.
10. The later Deuteronomy drafts make much stronger historical claims than the published Covenant Code article. The latter's synchronic argument must not be cited as establishing every claim of single authorship, lost transmission or exclusion of oral composition in those drafts.

## Source-specific checks to preserve before drafting

These are review notes, not changes to the author's documents.

- *Ways of Holiness*: "darkness" as clinical depression is an exploratory analogy, not the translation of Exodus 10. Its locust summary repeats the overly broad claim that Moses points at the sky for all three final signs. Check against the individual instructions.
- Its Leviticus 19 table occasionally omits part of 19:12 and labels the last cell `3M` where the argument uses `3R`. Use the complete marked biblical text, not a shortened analytical quotation, as the base.
- Its account of the enslaved woman introduces consent, a one-night encounter and the man's motives beyond what the biblical passage states. Those embellishments are not evidence for a structural reading.
- *Beautiful Weave*: the absolute assertion that איש disappears after Pair 2 is contradicted by 24:11–12 (Pair 3) and 24:16 (Pair 4). A change of emphasis may remain, but absence does not.
- The claim that ובערת הרע מקרבך occurs only in Pair 2 overlooks 22:21 and 22:24 in Pair 1. These appear in the draft's own appendix.
- The claim that divine love/emotional vocabulary occurs only in Pair 1 needs qualification: 23:6 explicitly says YHWH loves Israel, and the draft later uses that fact.
- "House of YHWH" is not unique to Deuteronomy 23:19 in the Torah's laws: Exodus 23:19 and 34:26 use בית יהוה אלהיך. The local shift to a house can still be interpreted without asserting uniqueness.
- Walking about does not lexically establish homelessness. The proposed narrative of divine displacement, shared dwelling and marriage is an interpretation of the selected relationships, not the bare sense of מתהלך.
- The document alternates between nineteen occurrences and nineteen *other* occurrences of the chosen-place expression. Count the Hebrew forms under a stated rule before using the number.
- Distinguish a legal analogy (rape compared to murder) from a general license or command to allegorize every law. The millstone/womb derivation and the legal identification of the captive with שפחה חרופה need independent support.
- References to the complete Covenant Code sometimes mean Exodus 21:1–23:19 and sometimes only the 5×3 portion at 22:17–23:19. Preserve the published article's scope when citing it.
- Pair 4's main-text interest quotation omits 23:20 and misnumbers 23:21; the appendix supplies the fuller passage. A Pair 1 inheritance reference is mislabeled `1RB` instead of `1LB`.
- Forty paragraph divisions and their visual symbolism require checking the actual marked Hebrew edition. The draft itself moves between categorical proof and an explicit open question about the forty lashes connection.
- Agricultural order supports an organized reading; uncalculated claims of zero probability or impossibility of an editor are not demonstrated by that observation alone.

The March/April 2018 conversation transcripts preserve valuable distinctions between demonstration and exploration:

- Exodus III: Brett explicitly notices that the locust instruction says to stretch the hand over the **land**. Moshe acknowledges it and distinguishes the instruction from the locusts' arrival by wind. Thus "all three gestures point skyward" is not even the considered conclusion of this discussion. Aaron's staff/Moses' hand is a contrast in instructions, not a claim that Moses never uses a staff.
- The public/private and outer/skin/inner reading of the three final-column signs is interpretive. Darkness as depression is an analogy. Frogs producing lice is attributed to someone else's explanation, not demonstrated as the textual mechanism. The transcript explicitly cannot settle why the explanatory statement is at 4B rather than the center.
- The proposed political progression is useful but its retelling needs correction: Exodus 11:8 speaks of Pharaoh's **servants** coming and bowing, not necessarily Pharaoh himself. Claims of no damage or suffering in the first triad are too categorical (the fish die and the water stinks).
- Exodus X: determine its outer boundaries first; then use repeated sending language to test the three divisions. The book lies between the two applications of blood in 2B, connecting altar and people. The three levels of recipients/revelation and the contrast between leadership and divine experience emerge through the whole table. Whether engagement with the community is a prerequisite for revelation is raised as an interpretation, not given as a literal clause.
- Moshe explicitly declines to push the horizontal/vertical account of the three divider units too far. The transcript also declines a direct A-to-A alignment between Exodus and Numbers. Respect these limits.
- The assertion that angels occur only in Exodus V, X and XV overlooks Exodus 3:2 in Unit I. A narrower claim about the angel **leading the camp** may be supported; the broader absence claim is not.
- The Genesis map conversation mistakes Rachel's burial for Hebron and doubts Ishmael's death at 137. Genesis 35:19 and 25:17 resolve these points. In the early unit guides, Eve also says Elohim (3:3), Cain builds the city named after Enoch (4:17), and Eve is not explicitly called cursed. These are notes for source checking, not changes to the source documents.
- The editor's comments make the intended accessibility problem concrete: readers need the map and unit text, translations of Hebrew terminology, and an explanation of location in the book before being asked to follow the comparisons.
