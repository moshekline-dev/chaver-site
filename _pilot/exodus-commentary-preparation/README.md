## Governing approach

The task is to learn the author's practice from the Genesis commentaries and methodological essays before writing Exodus. These notes distinguish the author's interpretations from independently checked textual observations. Reading an assertion in a commentary is not verification of its Hebrew evidence. Existing source files have not been edited.

The main continuity to investigate is the movement from the family constituted in Genesis, with YHWH increasingly unrecognized within Elohim's operations, to the revelation of YHWH and the construction of a dwelling among Israel. The signs distinguish YHWH through disruption; the sanctuary permits a continuing presence through material contributions and human work. Holiness is essential to the distinction between mere absorption of YHWH into Elohim and their revealed conjunction. This is a working question supplied by the sources, not a conclusion to impose on every Exodus unit.

## Principles to carry forward

1. **Find the units through converging evidence.** Boundaries, introductory formulas, closures, changes of setting and internal organization must support one another. Neither conventional chapters nor a desired numerical pattern establishes a unit.
2. **Read a true table in two dimensions.** Rows and columns need independently intelligible organizing principles. A passage must make sense at their intersection, not merely resemble another passage somewhere in the book.
3. **Follow the evidence into interpretation.** Establish the formal relationship, identify exact shared language, then ask what the difference between paired passages communicates. A similar setting frequently makes a difference more significant.
4. **Explain position.** Why is this passage here? Why this neighbor or correspondent? What would be lost if the order or position changed? A catalogue of repetitions is not a commentary.
5. **Keep linear and spatial readings together.** Narrative sequence matters, even when the weave adds relationships unavailable from sequence alone. Distinguish reading order, layout and a proposed order of composition.
6. **Read at several scales.** Pericope, row, column, unit, paired units, book and Torah-wide structures can supply distinct contexts. Do not jump to the whole-Torah interpretation before establishing the local argument.
7. **Attend to the conceptual middle.** A middle can mediate opposing terms by sharing a different feature with each. Demonstrate both connections. Do not assume that every physical center has an identical function.
8. **Retain anomalies.** Envelopes, unequal rows and independent units can be meaningful. Explain the exception within its actual structure rather than regularizing it away. The ten irregular units and seven independent units are different categories.
9. **Distinguish divine names and voices.** Preserve YHWH, Elohim, haElohim and compound forms. Record grammatical subject and whether an occurrence belongs to narration, direct divine speech, human testimony, recalled speech or an angelic message. Character knowledge is not narrator knowledge.
10. **Verify the Hebrew.** Check roots, conjugations, prepositions, repeated formulas, exact positions and domain of any absence claim. Mark thematic analogies separately from author-highlighted verbal connections. English synonyms are not evidence of the same Hebrew word.
11. **Let the reader discover.** Begin with a real textual puzzle; present the arrangement and evidence; develop its meaning in connected prose; return to the puzzle with a better reading. The reader's participation is part of the method.
12. **Separate levels of certainty.** Observable arrangement, literary interpretation and historical reconstruction are distinct claims. Egyptian, philosophical and later mystical comparisons need their own evidence. They do not establish local biblical boundaries.

## Voice and presentation

Use conversational scholarship, precise quotations and qualified interpretive language. Explain technical terms when they are needed. Prefer we/us as an invitation to inspect the evidence. Avoid hype, computational metaphors and lists that replace an argument. Use deliverance, rescue or preservation as appropriate; preserve the specific divine name instead of generic God in analytical prose. Follow the current project instructions where older essays use different terminology.

Unit notation: row numeral, uppercase column, lowercase subdivision, for example 2Ba. Distinguish unit numbers from biblical chapters throughout. State the versification being used, particularly where Hebrew and English editions differ.

## Genesis as preparation

Correspondents include 5/12, 7/14, 9/16 on the covenant tracks and 6/11, 8/13, 10/15 on the family tracks. The divine-name row scheme belongs to Genesis; it is not a template to impose on Exodus.

## Exodus structure to investigate

The existing Exodus map gives four quads and three independent units. Its quads are Egypt (1–4), wilderness/Sinai and social law (6–9), sanctuary instructions (11–14), and execution (16–19). Independent Units 5, 10 and 15 concern the sea, covenant confirmation, and calf/renewal respectively. Unit 10 is the pivot between the nine preceding units and the nine following units.

## Required sequence before commentary drafting

First complete the source/version register. Then read all nineteen marked Exodus units, inspecting actual table coordinates, subdivisions, highlighted vocabulary and boundaries. Build an evidence sheet for each. Read the book map in both narrative directions and test the suggested quad correspondences. Read any genuinely corresponding Genesis units and all relevant method discussions in full. Only then develop the Exodus equivalents of the Genesis foundation essays: identification of units, map, structural dimensions and interpretation of the architecture. A unit commentary follows those foundations and its own evidence sheet.

For every proposed parallel, record both passages, Hebrew wording, coordinates, marker class (if any), similarity, decisive difference, interpretive consequence and confidence. A thematic analogy must not be described as an author-marked lexical connection.

## What the methodological articles add

The Decalogue studies make the demand for two independent axes explicit. Pairing alone does not establish a weave. The five row themes and the distinction between the two columns must together explain each Word. Their analyses of the first Word and the Sabbath Word also demonstrate that the same organizing principle may recur at different scales. A resemblance between scales becomes useful when it clarifies a particular textual feature; resemblance alone is insufficient.

The Avot studies show how an initial description can be refined during reading. The Maharal's love/fear distinction is subsequently reconsidered as inward/outward or intrinsic/extrinsic. These are stages of an argument, not interchangeable labels to attach at will. A successful refinement explains more of the text, including its unusual expansions and changes of speaker. The progression from obvious repetition through shared form to a contextual inference teaches the reader how to discover a composition.

The Men of Kfar Hananya supplies a particularly useful model for Exodus: first establish the eight components and their parallel cycles; then distinguish the common subject of each pair from the constant difference between columns; finally interpret the transition across the whole. Its interpretation of an encounter with the divine as a passage toward responsible action offers a comparison for Exodus, not an automatic conclusion about it.

Avot 4 demonstrates the need to establish the hierarchy of a complex structure before discussing its meaning. Double statements identify boundaries; the resulting 5–10–5 arrangement is examined before the middle ten are read in pairs and the four groups of five compared. A rotated display must preserve the actual reading order and coordinate identities. The interpretation of Shmuel HaKatan returns to the opening puzzle by explaining why the speaker quotes rather than adding words. This is a model of a commentary earning its conclusion through position.

The Leviticus 19 studies develop an initially plausible religious/ethical distinction into a more precise account of private/social orientation. They also insist on accounting for apparently leftover material. The 2008 published article's additional discussion of 19:19b–25 is especially important: material that interrupts an attractive pattern must be investigated rather than omitted. Its proposed integration remains an interpretation to test against the text.

The 2015 *Structure Is Theology* study expands context from pericope to unit, triad, ring and book. It treats the reader's movement through the text as consequential: the architecture can organize a process rather than merely depict a static building. For Exodus, this means testing what changes between instructions and execution, and what the calf and renewal make possible, rather than merely listing matching furnishings.

The earlier *Elohim and YHWH* study distinguishes deity on stage from deity described by another speaker. Its closing discussion of Exodus 3:13–15 explicitly connects the names to enduring deity and revelation in human history. This is a central interpretive question to bring to Exodus. Its attribution of particular clauses, proposed etymologies and universal claims still require direct textual checking.

The 2022 Covenant Code handout supplies a concrete starting point for Unit 9: five triads, with a mundane-oriented column, a mediating column and a YHWH-oriented column. Its fourth row places witnesses, adversaries and judges in correspondence, linking practical help for an opponent to judicial fairness. The published 2025 article has subsequently been found in the OneDrive archive and read in full. Its fuller argument and qualifications are recorded in [archive findings](archive-findings.md).

## Practical standard for the eventual prose

Each unit commentary should make one coherent argument. Begin with the specific difficulty that the arrangement resolves. Give readers enough of the marked text to inspect the arrangement themselves. Establish the strongest correspondence before explaining the less obvious one. Then show how the difference between counterparts produces meaning. Broaden to the book only after that local argument is secure. End by returning to the opening difficulty and stating exactly what the structural reading has explained.

The preparatory evidence sheet for a unit should contain:

- Its complete range, opening and closing evidence, hierarchy of rows and subdivisions, and reading order.
- The proposed rule for each axis, tested against every cell, including exceptions.
- Exact Hebrew correspondences and their actual marker classes: `horizontal1`, `vertical1`, `closure`, `ciasm1` and `ciasm2` where present.
- Speaker and grammatical-subject records for divine names, with narrator knowledge separated from character knowledge.
- Relevant previous units, corresponding units and Genesis passages, with both similarities and consequential differences.
- A short account of what position explains that a sequential reading alone leaves unresolved.
- Unresolved evidence and competing interpretations, kept distinct from the proposed commentary's conclusions.

## Important unresolved checks

- **Reunion versus concealment:** the Genesis architecture essays, Akedah essay, Unit 16 and Sixth Book do not frame Jacob's vow identically. The Sixth Book supplies a useful distinction between natural provision that conceals YHWH and holiness that reveals YHWH together with Elohim. Preserve the development rather than flattening it.
- **Divine-name row exceptions:** do not claim both names act in every middle-row unit. Unit 13 explicitly describes human invocation without divine action. Do not say Jacob never mentions YHWH again without addressing his prayer and Genesis 49:18.
- **Manna:** one Akedah passage attributes bread to Elohim, whereas other methodological passages explicitly treat manna as YHWH's provision. Verify the Exodus narrative before adopting either formulation.
- **Genesis Unit 18:** the Sixth Book's summary table says 5×3; the current unit commentary gives 6×3. Some collected versions also differ at 47:26/27. Verify against the marked table; do not copy the summary dimensions.
- **Creation notation:** an older essay calls the expanded unit 5×2; the current commentary describes 3×2 with the middle row subdivided into three. These can describe different hierarchical levels. State the level.
- **Tree/tablet correspondences:** the Creation/Decalogue essays and the current Genesis Unit 1 commentary assign life/knowledge associations differently. Verify which comparison is being made; do not transfer a column label mechanically between structures.
- **Grammar and etymology:** haElohim's interpretation, Moriah's proposed roots, yireh as revelation, and sound links such as shechin/shachan need philological checks. Similar consonants do not establish the same root.
- **Second tablets:** distinguish what Exodus narrates from details supplied in Deuteronomy and later traditions, including concealment, the ark, authorship of inscription and priestly pronunciation of the name.
- **Egyptian grid and rotating diamonds:** the local Egyptian-grid essay is explicitly a review draft; the rotation diary is exploratory. Keep these proposals separate from the published quad map and from independently established Hebrew evidence.
- **Historical assertions:** a coherent final-form architecture does not by itself prove a particular date, sole author, borrowing relationship or transmission history.
- **Decalogue divisions:** the current Mishnah-series *Esoteric Decalogue* contains prose reversing the Masoretic/rabbinic treatment of the opening Words; its displayed table and earlier Decalogue papers give a different account. Use the actual divisions and the table, and verify them, before explaining the numbering in Exodus.
- **Summary-table accuracy:** some Avot 4 comparison tables misattribute statements that the surrounding prose attributes correctly. The 2015 Leviticus article also gives inconsistent Exodus history/tabernacle boundaries in prose and diagram. Do not inherit these discrepancies into new arguments.
